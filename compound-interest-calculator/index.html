Frequently asked questions

What is the compound interest formula?

A = P(1 + r/n)nt, where P is the principal, r the annual rate as a decimal, n the number of compounds per year, and t the years. For example: ₹10,000 at 8% compounded yearly for 2 years = 10,000 × 1.08² = ₹11,664.

How does compounding frequency change the result?

More frequent compounding earns slightly more, because interest starts earning interest sooner. ₹10,000 at 8% for 2 years grows to ₹11,664 yearly-compounded but about ₹11,729.16 monthly-compounded.

What is the effective annual rate?

It is the equivalent yearly rate when compounding is taken into account: (1 + r/n)n − 1. For 8% compounded monthly it is about 8.30% — useful for comparing offers with different frequencies.
